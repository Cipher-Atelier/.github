# Cipher Atelier

Much remains unsolved, and humanity still has many discoveries ahead. AI can help us explore those questions, compare evidence, and test ideas. Careful sources, reproducible methods, and human judgment make that work meaningful.

Cipher Atelier is a place to build a research community around that curiosity. Founded by [Maxim Egorov](https://github.com/cayde-6), it begins with historical ciphers and welcomes questions beyond letters: manuscripts, inscriptions, unfamiliar writing systems, and other unresolved records where evidence can be studied responsibly.

Cipher Atelier is an informal research community, not a company. We claim no ownership of participants’ work or discoveries. Authorship, credit and rights remain with the respective creators and rights holders; collaborative work credits each person’s contribution.

## Contributions to HCPortal

Our [Anna Tichá postcard research](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/anna-ticha-cipher-postcards) is represented in [HCPortal’s AI decipherments register](https://ai.hcportal.eu/register/?q=Maxim+Egorov&size=all#register): **154 card entries credit “Maxim Egorov / Cipher-Atelier”**, with links to our readings and recorded methods (checked 30 October 2026). [HCPortal](https://hcportal.eu/about.html) provides historical-cipher databases, analysis tools and educational resources, developed since 2016.

[Eugen Antal](https://hcportal.eu/contributors.html) of the Slovak University of Technology in Bratislava manually reviewed the transcriptions and Czech readings of **cards 320 and 321**. His corrections are incorporated in the published texts; the [review record](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/anna-ticha-cipher-postcards/blob/main/research-updates/2026-10-29-antal-review/README.md) explains the changes and remaining questions. Register inclusion credits the contribution; it does not validate all 154 readings, which include partial and provisional proposals.

## Read the evidence

New to historical ciphers? The [research index](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index) shows what can currently be read, what remains uncertain, and how to check one example without programming. Each investigation separates source evidence, literal output and interpretation. Passing a replay checks the declared files and calculations; it does not establish historical truth.

## Explore and contribute

The [research index](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index) introduces nine investigations, their partial or conditional results, and open questions.

Follow [Start here](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index/blob/main/START_HERE.md) to propose an investigation, join existing work, or improve documentation. You can contribute through public issues and pull requests without organization membership. Maxim reviews proposals and decides whether to create a public repository.

The [contribution guide](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) covers coordination, evidence, source rights, credit and AI disclosure. Follow the [community code of conduct](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/.github/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## Join the community

For founder contact or to apply for membership, email [maxim.egorov.dev@gmail.com](mailto:maxim.egorov.dev@gmail.com) privately.

In your private membership application, include your contact email address, a link to your GitHub profile, and a brief explanation of why you want to join. Send these details only by email; do not post your contact email in a public issue.

Maxim reviews applications and decides invitations manually. A meaningful contribution may also lead to an invitation. Membership does not automatically grant repository write or administrator access. Participation is free; no review time is promised.
