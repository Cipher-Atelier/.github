# Contributing to Cipher-Atelier

Thank you for helping make a question clearer, a result easier to check, or a source easier to understand. Contributions can be small. You do not need organization membership, a particular credential, or a complete solution.

## Start with one question

1. Read the project’s README, sources, and open issues.
2. Choose a bounded question or improvement.
3. Open an issue for a source lead, proposed experiment, or interpretation. For an edit, fork the repository and submit a pull request.
4. Explain what changed, why it matters, and how someone else can check it.

Use the [research index](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index) to find a project. Introductions and membership interest belong in its community introduction issue. The founder, [Maxim Egorov](https://github.com/cayde-6), decides invitations manually. Issues and pull requests are open to contributors without organization membership. There is no participation fee, automatic role, or promised review time.

## Keep the evidence visible

For a research contribution, include what is relevant:

- The exact source: institution or collection, shelfmark or catalogue identifier, stable link, and page, image, line, or coordinate.
- The source transcription, with uncertain signs and alternatives preserved.
- Your proposed reading or explanation, clearly separated from the literal output.
- The method, assumptions, inputs, and commands needed to reproduce the result.
- What was tested, what failed, and what remains unresolved.
- Earlier keys, readings, editions, code, datasets, and researchers that informed the work.

Distinguish an archival source, a transcription, a hypothesis, an editorial repair, and a translation. Do not silently replace uncertain signs with letters that make a sentence fluent. Keep corrections traceable to the previous version.

A source link can be a useful contribution on its own. Explain what it might establish without claiming you have inspected evidence you have not seen.

## Make experiments checkable

Record the exact data and code version, relevant dependencies, parameters, and random seeds. Give a minimal command or procedure and the expected output.

Where training and heldout material are used, state what was visible before the split, what was frozen, when the heldout material was inspected, and any later corrections. A consumed holdout is not a fresh test. Synthetic-control scores, mapped-position coverage, and historical accuracy are different quantities.

Keep negative results and model limits. Say which finite family was tested; avoid claiming that every possible explanation has been excluded.

If essential evidence cannot be shared, state that limitation. Do not describe the package as fully reproducible.

## Use AI transparently

AI can assist with transcription, research, coding, analysis, and writing. State its relevant role, the model or tool when known, and how you checked its output. Include prompts or settings when they materially affect reproducibility and are safe to share.

Check citations, source readings, and code. Agreement between models is not independent human expert review. A fluent reconstruction, a successful round trip, or a high model score is not enough to verify a historical reading.

## Respect sources and rights

Only include material you have the right to contribute. Credit the source and record item-specific rights or permission where known. Keep code, original writing, transcriptions, datasets, and source images distinguishable.

A repository’s licence does not automatically cover archival scans or other third-party material. Do not add or change a licence for someone else’s work. If permission to redistribute an item is uncertain or restricted, link to the authorized provider and explain the source gap.

Do not upload private correspondence, personal contact details, credentials, restricted scans, or unpublished material without the appropriate authorization. All issues and pull requests in these public repositories are public.

## Work well together

Follow the [code of conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). Critique a claim with evidence, welcome corrections, and make disagreement useful. Ask before undertaking a large reorganization, and keep unrelated changes out of a pull request.

Review may lead to questions, revisions, or a decision not to merge. A merged contribution records work accepted into the project; it does not by itself certify a historical claim.
